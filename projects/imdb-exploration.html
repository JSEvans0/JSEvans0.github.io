Exploring IMDb Movies

A self-directed dig through 13.5 million rows of public film data.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • Data Cleaning
  • Visualization
  • Large Datasets
Code on GitHub

The problem

This one had no brief. I picked the dataset because I wanted to know what IMDb has actually collected across decades of film, and what a person who likes movies could learn from it. Specifically: does anything measurable predict whether a movie is rated well?

Choosing my own question was the point of the exercise. Nobody handed me a target variable.

The data

Two of the seven files from the IMDb Non-Commercial Datasets: title.basics.tsv and title.ratings.tsv. Between them they carry title type, primary and original title, adult flag, start and end year, runtime, up to three genres, average rating, and vote count.

Cleaning the data

The dataset covers every title type IMDb tracks — TV episodes dominate the row count by an enormous margin — so most of the work was getting from 13.5 million rows to the subset that could answer the question.

  1. Merged the two tables on the title identifier.
  2. Filtered 13,520,995 rows down to the 725,597 movies, dropping episodes, shorts, TV series and video titles.
  3. Dropped endYear, which only applies to series.
  4. Converted startYear and runtimeMinutes to numeric types.
  5. Dropped rows with nulls — 185,802 remained.
  6. Dropped rows where genres was IMDb’s \N placeholder — 174,050 clean movie records remained.

13.5M → 174,050. Knowing which 1.3% of the data actually answers your question is most of the job. The other 98.7% isn’t bad data — it just isn’t about movies.

What I found

  • The 90-minute movie is the standard. Runtime clusters hard at 90 minutes; it’s far and away the most common length.
  • The most common rating is 7.2 out of 10, but the mean across all movies is 6.5 — the distribution has a long tail of poorly rated films pulling the average down below the mode.
  • Runtime has no meaningful relationship to rating. Longer movies are not better-rated movies.
  • Vote count has no meaningful relationship to rating either. Popularity and quality, at least as IMDb measures them, are close to independent. A film with a hundred thousand votes is no more likely to be well rated than one with a few hundred.

Why it matters

The two null results are the interesting part. “Long movies are self-indulgent” and “popular means overrated” are both things people say confidently, and neither one survives contact with the data. Being able to answer that with a number rather than an opinion is the whole reason to look.

What I’d do differently

Genre is the obvious next axis — ratings almost certainly vary more between genres than they do by runtime, and each title carries up to three of them, so it needs proper handling rather than a single column. I’d also look at rating trends by release decade, and weight average rating by vote count to see whether low-vote titles are adding noise to the distribution.
